1 — Engineering Ownership · Session 02

Reviewing AI-Generated Artifacts

🚧 DRAFT 🚧

Build a deliberate review habit that starts with intent and risk before inspecting implementation detail.

Estimated time: 45–55 minutes · Initial implementation; validation is still pending.

1. Learning Objectives

  • Review AI output in risk-first passes.
  • Separate correctness, maintainability, security, cost, and operability concerns.
  • Identify unsupported assumptions and suspicious omissions.
  • Write review findings that are specific and actionable.

2. Before You Begin

Write a one-sentence answer before continuing:

If AI produced a change that passed a quick check, what would you still need to own?

3. The Concept

1. Review intent before syntax

First restate what the change must accomplish and what it must not change. Correct-looking code can still solve the wrong problem.

2. Review in passes

Use focused passes for boundaries, behavior, failure paths, security, cost, maintainability, and operations. A checklist supports judgment; it does not replace it.

3. Look for omissions

AI output often appears complete while leaving out cancellation, timeouts, authorization, migrations, rollback, resource limits, or error handling. Missing behavior can carry more risk than visible defects.

4. Make findings testable

A useful finding names the location, consequence, triggering condition, and a proportionate next action. “This seems wrong” is not enough for another engineer to verify.

Working rule: AI output is a candidate artifact. Confidence comes from engineering evidence, not fluency or speed.

4. Lab

Exercise

Review a short AI-generated change in two passes. Pass one may inspect only the requirement and public behavior. Pass two inspects the implementation. Produce at least one finding in three different risk categories, then rank findings by consequence rather than style preference.

5. Expected Evidence

  • Restated acceptance criteria
  • Pass-by-pass review notes
  • At least three actionable findings
  • A consequence-based priority order

6. Progress Checkpoint

7. Code Review Checklist

8. Reflection Questions

  • What did the intent-first pass reveal?
  • Which omission was hardest to notice?
  • Where did personal preference try to masquerade as correctness?

9. What Engineering Concept Was Learned Today?

The engineer—not the generator—must connect intent, artifact, consequence, and evidence.
